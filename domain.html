Domain Overview

The academic foundations behind SmartAgri: literature review, research gaps, problem statement, objectives, methodology, system components, and technology stack.

01

Literature Survey

Agriculture forms the vital backbone of Sri Lanka's rural economy, yet smallholder farmers face severe productivity and economic vulnerabilities. Global precision agriculture research has increasingly deployed machine learning classifiers, IoT sensor telemetry, and predictive market models.

However, traditional systems remain heavily fragmented: crop prediction tools operate independently from soil nutrient assessment, IoT telemetry nodes function as high-cost standalone logging systems, and market price models analyze historical commodity statistics without offering actionable planting advice.

SmartAgri shifts precision agriculture into an integrated, accessible developer and farmer ecosystem, combining real-time edge telemetry with localized agronomic prediction models tailored specifically to Sri Lankan agro-ecological zones and monsoon cycles (Yala and Maha).

02

Research Gap

🔍

Fragmented Information Silos

Prior solutions evaluate crop suitability, soil health, fertiliser dosages, and market demand in isolation, forcing farmers to interpret disjointed sources.

🎯

Lack of Local Agro-Climatic Sensitivity

Global machine learning models rely on temperate foreign agricultural datasets, failing to capture Sri Lankan district microclimates and seasonal shifts.

🧪

Static Non-Adaptive Fertiliser Recipes

Chemical fertiliser advisories employ static lookup recommendations without real-time telemetry of existing soil N-P-K mineral levels, causing over-fertilisation.

📊

Inactionable Wholesale Market Data

Historical price records do not provide forward-looking harvest timing advice, leaving smallholder farmers vulnerable to severe market price crashes.

03

Problem & Objectives

Problem Statement: Smallholder farmers across rural Sri Lanka suffer significant crop losses, escalating input expenditures, and erratic revenue due to the lack of timely, synchronized, and accessible decision support.

Main Research Objective: To engineer and empirically validate an integrated, AI- and IoT-driven Smart Agriculture Support System delivering multi-variable crop recommendations, continuous soil-health telemetry, tailored nutrient & fertiliser optimization, and market intelligence.

Specific Objectives:

1
Crop Viability Modelling:

Develop a Random Forest multi-class classifier using district rainfall, temperature, and soil indicators to recommend optimal crops with cultivation guidance.

2
Nutrient & Fertiliser Optimizer:

Construct a predictive model to quantify Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), and Potassium (K) deficits and compute balanced fertiliser formulations.

3
Soil Health IoT Telemetry:

Architect low-cost microcontroller edge sensor nodes to continuously capture moisture, pH, and temperature with automated distress alerts.

4
Agricultural Market Intelligence:

Implement time-series price forecasting capturing wholesale trends at regional economic centers to optimize harvest selling periods.

04

Methodology

The research employs a quantitative, experimental engineering methodology structured across 4 synchronized phases to guarantee system stability and model accuracy.

PHASE 01

Data Collection & Telemetry

Acquiring regional agro-climatic datasets from national meteorological repositories and streaming field sensor telemetry via IoT edge microcontroller probes.

PHASE 02

Preprocessing & Feature Engineering

Normalising continuous variables (temperature, humidity, pH, N-P-K ratios), handling telemetry outliers, and encoding seasonal attributes.

PHASE 03

Model Training & Cross-Validation

Training Random Forest and ensemble regression algorithms with K-Fold cross-validation to maximize accuracy without overfitting.

PHASE 04

System Integration & Web Deployment

Connecting edge sensor telemetry, model inference microservices, and client portal within a lightweight, responsive interface.

Evaluation Framework: System validation includes Classification Accuracy, F1-Scores, Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), R² coefficients, and sensor telemetry latency benchmarks.

06

Technologies Used

The technological stack is specifically selected to guarantee computational efficiency, low latency, and adherence to CDAP guidelines:

🤖

AI & Machine Learning

Python 3.10+, Scikit-Learn, Pandas, NumPy, Random Forest Classifiers, Regression Ensembles, and K-Fold Cross-Validation.

⚡

IoT & Hardware Telemetry

ESP32 / NodeMCU 32-bit Microcontrollers, Capacitive Soil Moisture Sensors, RS485 NPK Soil Probes, and MQTT / HTTP Communication Protocols.

☁️

Backend & API Services

Flask / FastAPI microservices exposing model inference endpoints, SQLite historical telemetry databases, and JSON data pipelines.

🌐

Frontend & Visualization

HTML5, Modern CSS3 variables, Vanilla JavaScript, Chart.js for data visualization, and fully responsive accessible layouts.
